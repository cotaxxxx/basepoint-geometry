Dear Editors of Experimental Mathematics,

Please consider the manuscript “A Certified Stationary-Orbit Bifurcation for a Cone-Volume-Weighted Radial–Normal Angle on Prolate Spheroids” for publication as a Research Article in Experimental Mathematics.

The paper studies a geometric functional in which the base point is the variable. Numerical exploration suggested that, as the axis ratio of a prolate spheroid varies, an equatorial stationary circle contracts into the center at a critical parameter. The manuscript turns that observation into a rigorous local bifurcation theorem. The critical axis ratio is certified to a 20-decimal interval, the relevant quadratic and quartic signs are proved by outward-rounded ball arithmetic, and an independent axial positivity certificate closes the three-dimensional stationary condition.

The experimental and rigorous stages are deliberately separated. Exploratory calculations are used only to locate and formulate the phenomenon. The proof relies on a public twelve-object certificate chain containing symbolic audits, Arb/Acb interval certificates, JSON reports, and a SHA-256 manifest. At the canonical public commit abf704b8701f3da8eb41d8a99408570a413b2214, all twelve proof objects verify against the manifest. The symbolic audits also check the regularized hypergeometric derivative chain and the raw axial kernel independently of the numerical integration code.

I believe the manuscript fits Experimental Mathematics because the experiment leads directly to a formal theorem, while the computational evidence required by the proof is reported in a form intended to be independently repeatable and human-auditable.

The result is local and the manuscript states this scope explicitly; no global classification of all stationary components is claimed. The manuscript is original and is submitted for consideration as a research article.
